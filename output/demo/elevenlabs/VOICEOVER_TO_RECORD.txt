[1. Hook]

One drone flight. Hundreds of rooftops. A whole neighbourhood of possibilities.

Now imagine being able to measure that neighbourhood: the width of a road, the area of a roof, even the volume of a stockpile. All from the video you just captured.

Meet DRISHTI-3D.


[2. The working product (MVP)]

Built for NTRO's single-pass reconstruction challenge, DRISHTI turns drone video and flight data into a georeferenced 3D model.

Here's our working MVP. Load the video, add the flight log, and hit Run. The application selects useful frames, solves the camera positions, and builds the model as you watch.

Then you can explore it, measure it, and export the results—all inside the same desktop application.


[3. Our own engine]

The exciting part is the engine we built underneath.

We developed our own camera-optimisation solver and GPU stereo implementation, designed around the challenges of a single flight path.

Our custom solver made the camera-optimisation steps around thirteen times faster than our earlier implementation on the benchmark flight.

We also tested against COLMAP, the standard research tool, on the same laptop. COLMAP took thirty-three minutes for a sparse model. DRISHTI delivered a dense, textured, georeferenced model in under ten minutes.

And where the camera position is exact, our surface landed almost a metre closer to the surveyed points than COLMAP's.

That's the engineering behind our promise: turn captured footage into usable geometry, with evidence for what the model has measured.


[4. How the architecture works]

Here's how it comes together.

First, video and telemetry are aligned, and sharp, overlapping frames are selected.

LightGlue connects matching features across those frames. Our bundle-adjustment solver then refines the cameras and lens parameters using image evidence and GPS.

Next, our GPU stereo implementation estimates depth. Neighbouring views cross-check those estimates before Open3D fuses them into a textured surface.

The pipeline reports progress and previews to the desktop viewer while it runs. Georeferencing connects the finished model to real-world coordinates.


[5. Using it on the real model]

Now let's use it.

Switch to confidence colours to inspect reliability. Click across a road to measure distance. Outline a roof for its area. Check a volume, or trace an elevation profile.

Then take those measurements into your GIS workflow with GeoJSON or KML, alongside the exported models and maps.


[6. Evidence and unique value]

Our recorded reconstruction contains around eight million vertices. On a separate Spain benchmark, thirteen stable features showed twenty-nine-centimetre median horizontal agreement with the national reference orthophoto.

Our unique value brings these pieces together: our own reconstruction core, visible confidence on every surface, and practical measurement tools—from one flight to usable results.
